What should a detector do when the world looks unfamiliar?

I led an undergraduate research group exploring out-of-distribution object detection for driving scenes, combining a reproduced STUD baseline, dataset adaptation and adverse-weather experiment design.

01 / THE PROBLEM

A confident label can still be wrong.

Unknown objects

A conventional detector works with a defined set of classes. An unfamiliar object can receive a confident familiar label. Our project investigated how an unknown-aware detector could expose this uncertainty in driving-related scenes.

Changing visual conditions

Adverse weather adds a second challenge: familiar objects can look different in rain, fog or low visibility. We examined the data pipeline and designed weather-mixture experiments to study robustness under these changes.

02 / THE RESEARCH APPROACH

Reproduce, adapt, inspect.

My role combined research planning, environment setup, data preparation, qualitative experiments and the midterm presentation.

  1. 01

    Establish the baseline

    Study Spatial-Temporal Unknown Distillation (STUD) and reproduce its workflow using Python, PyTorch, CUDA and Detectron2.

  2. 02

    Adapt the datasets

    Prepare dataset formats, folder organisation and selected categories across BDD100K, COCO2017 and YouTube-VIS.

  3. 03

    Inspect detections

    Compare known and unknown detections in animal, outdoor-object and indoor-object examples. Retain both successes and failures.

  4. 04

    Prepare weather data

    Process adverse-weather imagery and define mixtures of extreme-weather, clear-weather, indoor and outdoor examples.

  5. 05

    Design controlled comparisons

    Vary the extreme-weather share while holding the training-set size fixed. Test the relationship between training composition and performance.

  6. 06

    Explore optimisation

    Investigate Pareto Invariant Risk Minimization (PAIR) as a possible robustness extension. Integration remained exploratory in the supplied materials.

REPRODUCED BASELINE

Learning from unknown candidates

STUD distils candidate unknown objects across video frames and uses an energy-based uncertainty objective to help separate known and unknown inputs. Our work built on this published method; the STUD architecture is credited to its original authors.

Practical work included dataset conversion and resolving compatibility problems between Detectron2 and the required environment.

EXPLORATORY EXTENSION

Balancing competing objectives

PAIR introduces a multi-objective approach to out-of-distribution generalisation. We investigated it as a way to balance learning objectives when extending the baseline to difficult weather conditions.

The implementation file is explicitly marked as not yet successfully integrated. This case study therefore presents PAIR as research exploration, with no measured gain attributed to it.

04 / DOCUMENTED FINDINGS

Useful successes. Informative failures.

The October 2024 report contains qualitative detection examples and records the next experimental steps.

QUALITATIVE OBSERVATION

Unknown-object recognition

The midterm example shows giraffes assigned an OOD label alongside a known pedestrian. It illustrates the intended separation of familiar and unfamiliar categories in that scene.

FAILURE CASE

Confidence is not correctness

The same example contains a rock labelled “train” at 71% confidence. This is a single detection score, not dataset accuracy, and demonstrates why confident predictions still need inspection.

RESEARCH PROGRESS

From reproduction to data design

The materials document a working reproduction, qualitative scene comparisons and adverse-weather data preparation. Selecting the best data ratio, tuning the optimisation and testing real traffic were listed as subsequent work.

What the available evidence supports

The contribution shown here is the research process: reproducing a method, adapting datasets, analysing failure cases and designing a robustness study. The supplied midterm materials do not include a verifiable final AUROC comparison, so no percentage improvement is claimed.

05 / REFLECTION

Robustness needs a careful experimental record.

What I took from the project

Reproducing a research method involves much more than running a model. Dataset structure, environment compatibility and inspection of failure cases all determine whether an experiment is interpretable.

What a stronger evaluation would add

A reproducible comparison would retain fixed data splits, training configurations and repeated runs, then report OOD detection measures alongside known-class detection performance. This would test whether weather adaptation improves robustness without sacrificing ordinary detection.
