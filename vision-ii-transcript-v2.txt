Inside the penalty kick.
Vision Two turns football penalty video into structured, reviewable evidence. This project connects computer vision with human judgement.
Source: Original project output / Colombia–England, kick 2 / 2018 World Cup broadcast

39 kicks. Four shoot-outs.
The dataset covers thirty-nine penalties from four twenty-eighteen World Cup shoot-outs. Shared clip identifiers connect model observations, human labels, and final feature tables.
Source: Vision II project dataset / human-confirmed labels

From pixels to features.
Pretrained YOLO eleven models extract observations. Optical flow compensates for camera motion, while motion-constrained beam search links ball detections. Human review resolves uncertain cases.
Source: Project workflow / pretrained models, task-specific rules and human review

Measure error. Keep the denominator.
Automatic counting produced thirty-five outputs, with four failures. Against manually confirmed counts, mean absolute error is two point six five seven steps and root mean squared error is three point three seven six. Four predictions match exactly; twelve are within one step.
Source: Project evaluation / errors evaluated only on the 35 successful automatic outputs

Make uncertainty visible.
Automatic quality checks accepted eighteen goalkeeper records and twenty-six ball trajectories. The remaining records require review. These acceptance counts measure quality-control status, not detection accuracy.
Source: Project QA exports / acceptance counts and review flags
