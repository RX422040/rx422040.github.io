01 / THE QUESTION

What can we reliably extract from a penalty video?

The project turns unstructured football broadcasts into a consistent set of observations: kicking foot and run-up steps; goalkeeper pre-kick activity, dive direction and height; ball trajectory and final image position; and the observed goal/no-goal result.

The core contribution is an auditable analysis workflow. It retains model observations, review flags and human labels separately, so each final feature can be traced back to its evidence. It uses pretrained models rather than training a new end-to-end network.

02 / THE WORKFLOW

One clip. A traceable chain of evidence.

Canonical clip IDs join the video, annotations, automatic outputs and final feature tables.

  1. 01

    Prepare the clips

    Slice four shoot-outs into 39 canonical clips. Record match, player, kick order, frame rate and resolution.

  2. 02

    Extract observations

    Detect the live-action camera segment, estimate 17 body keypoints and collect candidate person and ball tracks.

  3. 03

    Anchor the event

    Review the contact frame and identify the taker and goalkeeper. Use this shared time reference across the analyses.

  4. 04

    Derive the features

    Apply gait rules, camera stabilisation and trajectory search to estimate motion and behaviour.

  5. 05

    Review uncertainty

    Check frame-level evidence, preserve automatic predictions and record manual decisions in separate fields.

  6. 06

    Export and validate

    Join independent outcome labels. Export feature CSVs, diagnostic JSON and visual QA reports; verify IDs and row counts.

03 / METHODS

Four connected analyses.

A / PENALTY TAKER

From ankle motion to run-up steps.

YOLO11 Pose supplies hip and ankle keypoints. Hip-relative ankle trajectories are normalised by body scale and projected along the run-up direction. Peak and trough detection produces candidate footfalls.

  • Use temporal continuity and positional priors to select the taker.
  • Compare left/right ankle motion near contact to estimate the kicking foot.
  • Check alternating feet, minimum intervals and the final support-foot plant.
  • Merge frame-by-frame human footfall labels into the confirmed step count.
The counting rule

After intentional run-up begins, count each transition from swing to a new landing. Exclude a foot already planted at the start boundary; include the final non-kicking support-foot plant before contact. Exclude the ball-contact event and recovery landings.

B / GOALKEEPER

Separate player motion from camera motion.

Goal-area location, body scale and track continuity help identify the goalkeeper. Tracks are stitched across detector-ID interruptions, with a 960-pixel goal-region pose fallback for difficult cases.

  • Track Shi–Tomasi background corners with Lucas–Kanade optical flow.
  • Estimate a robust affine camera transform with RANSAC.
  • Calculate pre-kick movement from stabilised, body-normalised positions.
  • Describe dive direction and height using displacement after contact.
How to interpret the coordinates

Direction is image-left or image-right, not anatomical left or right. Body-height normalisation reduces scale differences but does not remove perspective distortion or provide a calibrated physical distance.

C / THE BALL

Score a trajectory across frames.

The tracker combines YOLO sports-ball candidates with an ankle-based contact anchor and appearance cues such as brightness, circularity and grass context. It searches for a coherent sequence rather than taking the highest-scoring detection in each frame.

  • Use velocity prediction and motion residuals to rank successive candidates.
  • Constrain continuity and motion toward the goal.
  • Use beam search to retain multiple plausible paths, tolerating short gaps.
  • Store path coverage, confidence, final position and review status.
What the trajectory represents

This is a two-dimensional image-plane trajectory. It is not a reconstructed 3D flight path, and the project does not use a Kalman filter. Speed is measured in pixels per second, not kilometres per hour.

D / OUTCOME

Keep the result independent of the tracker.

Goal/no-goal labels come from manually checked match outcomes, recorded with their source and confidence. They are joined to the feature tables by canonical clip ID.

  • Retain 26 observed goals and 13 no-goal outcomes.
  • Avoid inferring a goal from the last detected ball pixel.
  • Prevent a ball-tracking error from automatically changing the outcome label.
  • Keep more detailed outcome categories where available in the annotations.
Why a separate label matters

A final image position alone cannot reliably distinguish a goal, save, shot off target or woodwork contact. The binary outcome is useful for analysis, but it groups different mechanisms into the same no-goal category.

What this evaluation tells me

The workflow produces traceable features, but automatic run-up counting remains a weak component. The useful output is a reviewable dataset with explicit uncertainty, supported by retained visual evidence.

06 / REPRODUCIBILITY & ATTRIBUTION

Make the evidence inspectable.

Deliverables

A main analysis notebook, three 39-row feature tables, three diagnostic JSON exports, visual QA sheets, manual annotations, dependency specifications, run scripts and method documentation form the project handoff.

Checks enforce unique clip IDs, consistent joins and row counts, finite measurements and non-empty ball tracks. Unit tests exercise analysis components; passing software tests does not by itself establish measurement accuracy.

Implementation boundary

Ultralytics supplies pretrained object and pose models; OpenCV supplies video, image and motion-processing operations; SciPy supplies peak detection. Project-specific code handles player selection, event anchoring, step rules, goalkeeper features, trajectory scoring and review integration.

SoccerNet is related work at the task level, including tracking and game-state reconstruction. It is not presented here as the source of this project’s code or data. The project’s provenance audit found no direct SoccerNet dependency in the active workflow.

07 / LIMITS & NEXT STEPS

What I would strengthen next.

01

Independent ground truth

Add blinded second-reviewer step labels and frame-level ball-centre annotations. Report inter-reviewer agreement and trajectory error, beyond automatic QA acceptance.

02

Geometry and calibration

Use goal or pitch landmarks to establish a common coordinate system. Image normalisation alone cannot justify physical speed, distance or 3D trajectory claims.

03

Threshold stability

Test key movement and tracking thresholds at ±10% and ±20%. Evaluate on held-out clips to check whether rules generalise beyond the clips used during development.

04

Careful interpretation

Separate saves, off-target shots and woodwork where possible. Account for repeated players and goalkeepers in statistical analysis. Observed associations cannot establish causality.
